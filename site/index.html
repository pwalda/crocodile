Voice & text for friends and communities

Talk freely.
Nobody in between.

Hang out in spaces with text channels and voice rooms, or call a friend. Everything you say goes straight to the people you're talking to, end-to-end encrypted. No server ever reads it, and there's no phone number, email or password to hand over.

Private by design, friendly by default

Peer-to-peer

Voice and messages travel only between the people in the conversation. In groups, the member with the best connection passes on the encrypted audio, and a backup takes over if they leave.

End-to-end encrypted, post-quantum

Every message and every voice frame is encrypted on your device with keys that change constantly, and key exchange is hybrid with ML-KEM, so today's traffic stays safe tomorrow.

No account to lose

Pick a name and you're in. Your account is a key on your devices; add your laptop by typing a short code. Keep a recovery key in case you lose them all.

Rooms you can see into

Voice rooms show who's inside before you join. Push-to-talk works even while you're in a game. Light and dark themes, your accent colour.

Messages that wait for you

Wrote while your friend was offline? It waits on your device and goes out the moment you are both online. Optionally, a server can hold it for them, sealed so only they can read it.

Run by the community

Volunteer coordination servers only introduce people to each other. Anyone can run one — with Docker, or with a single switch in the app.

How it works

  1. Find the fastest server. The app asks this site's directory for the list of coordination servers and connects to the quickest, keeping the runner-up on standby.
  2. Meet your people. The server tells your app who's online and which member has the best connection to host a room. It sees who talks to whom — never what is said.
  3. Talk directly. Your app connects to the host (or straight to your friend) and everything flows peer-to-peer, encrypted end to end.

Behind a network that blocks direct connections? You can opt in to relaying your already-encrypted traffic through a server, an hour at a time. Security model →

Install

One line in a terminal installs the latest version, with no security prompts:

macOS & Linux curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pwalda/crocodile/main/scripts/install.sh | sh
Windows (PowerShell) irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pwalda/crocodile/main/scripts/install.ps1 | iex

Or grab an installer from the releases page. Until our builds are code-signed, Windows and macOS show a one-time warning for browser downloads: on Windows choose More info → Run anyway; on macOS open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. Microsoft Store and signed macOS builds are on the way.

Host a coordination server

Coordination servers are the phone operators of Crocodile: they connect calls but never listen in. Running one helps everyone near you get faster connections.

Docker docker run -d -p 7443:7443/tcp -p 7443:7443/udp -e CROC_PUBLIC_URL=https://croc.example.org ghcr.io/pwalda/crocodile-coordinator

Or open the app and turn on Settings → Host a server. Read the self-hosting guide.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. The app is free, and the servers are run by volunteers.

Can the servers read my messages?

No. Messages and voice never pass through coordination servers, except when you opt in to the relay or the mailbox — and even then they're end-to-end encrypted and unreadable to the server. Servers do see public profile information and who talks to whom.

What happens if my friend is offline?

Your message waits on your device and is delivered as soon as you're both online. If you turn on the mailbox, a server holds it for up to a few days, sealed so only your friend's devices can open it.

How do I use it on more than one computer?

On the new computer choose I already use Crocodile → Link from another device, then type the code it shows into a computer where you're signed in and check that both show the same security code.

Why does my computer warn me about the download?

Windows and macOS warn about apps that aren't code-signed yet. The one-line installers avoid the warning, and signed builds are coming.
